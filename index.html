Designing Sound for an Exhibition Experience

Imagine stepping into a gallery: paintings surround you, colors catch your eye, and each room has its own atmosphere. Now imagine adding sound. How might it change what you notice or feel? How can we design sound that supports the art and leaves space for your own interpretation?

In this workshop, you’ll create and try a soundtrack for a physical exhibition through a video walkthrough, then reconsider it after learning more about the art. No music-making experience is needed. Earphones or headphones are recommended, but optional. Follow the steps in order. Silence is also a valid design choice.

Step 1. Get ready

Open Suno Create and sign in. Ask the facilitator for workshop access if needed.

Step 2. Explore without sound

Open the exhibition walkthrough. Mute it and turn captions off. Keep the video muted throughout the workshop.

Watch the video for a while to get an overall impression of the exhibition. Feel free to speed up, pause, or skip forward to different sections. Focus on the paintings and space; avoid descriptions and outside research. If you recognize the artist, note what you already know.

Step 3. Describe your impressions

Set a one-minute timer and write as many words or short phrases as you can that describe the exhibition. When the minute ends, circle the three most important words - or type them in the box below. Keep your full list.

Rate immersion, emotional engagement, and coherence (how connected the exhibition feels), from 1 = very low to 5 = very high.

Step 4. Create your first soundtrack

In Suno, turn on Instrumental. Fill in this prompt:

Create an instrumental soundtrack for a physical art exhibition that feels [WORD 1], [WORD 2], and [WORD 3]. No vocals or spoken words.

Generate once. Listen to the results and choose a clip that can be downloaded; some results may not offer downloads. Open its three-dot menu → Download → Audio/MP3 and save it as YourName_Round1.mp3 (keep its actual file extension). Save your exact prompt and briefly note whether the sound matches your words. If none can be downloaded, ask the facilitator for help.

Step 5. Experience it with sound

Play your track in a separate tab or audio player at a comfortable volume. Browse the muted video for a while with the music playing. Feel free to switch sections and explore how the sound fits different parts of the exhibition.

Rate the same three qualities again. Briefly note: What changed? What fit, and what distracted you?

Step 6. Learn more and look again

Open the context below. Then stop your soundtrack and browse the video again silently. Feel free to explore different sections.

Round 2: exhibition context

Monet and Chicago. This Art Institute of Chicago exhibition explored the city’s early support for Claude Monet through collectors such as Bertha Honoré Palmer and Martin A. Ryerson. It tells a story about how art was collected and valued, as well as the artist’s work. Bertha Palmer and her collection Exhibition overview

Same subject, changing perception. Monet painted repeated subjects, including wheat stacks, under different light, weather, and seasons. What changes is how the subject appears. Explore a painting from the wheat-stack series

A garden he helped create. Monet developed his water garden at Giverny, then returned to it repeatedly in his paintings. He shaped the environment he observed. Water garden

Deliberate revision. Monet built up layers of paint and revised his water-lily compositions, including removing some lily pads. Explore layers and revisions in a related Water Lilies painting

What do you notice differently? Write a new word list and choose three key words. They may be the same as before.

Step 7. Reconsider your soundtrack

Choose one and briefly explain why:

  • KEEP: use your first track unchanged.
  • REVISE: adjust its sonic direction.
  • REPLACE: try a different direction.
  • REMOVE: use silence.

For REVISE or REPLACE, generate once using the same template with your new words. For REVISE, add one sentence describing the change. Choose from the results that can be downloaded. Download your selected clip from its three-dot menu and save it as YourName_Round2.mp3 (keep its actual file extension). Save the new prompt and keep your first audio file too. If none can be downloaded, ask the facilitator for help.

Step 8. Test your final choice

Browse the video again with your final track - or in silence. Feel free to switch sections. Rate the same three qualities again.

Briefly note: Did the context affect your choice? Where would you use sound, and where would you prefer silence? Why?

Step 9. Save and upload

Check that your downloaded audio files play and that their filenames include your name and round.
